TITLE

Optimal stimulation sites are not the most affected: personalised fMRI models of Alzheimer's disease


ABSTRACT

Targeted neuromodulation requires two decisions that are still made heuristically: which site to engage, and at what amplitude. We address both with individualised digital twins of brain dynamics. For each participant a reservoir-computing model is fit to resting-state functional MRI so that its free-running output reproduces that individual's own lagged connectivity, and we show that the fitted models are identifiable across individuals. Because the recurrent substrate is fixed and shared, and only a linear read-out is fit per person, the twins live in common coordinates: they can be compared, averaged and perturbed. Two interventions are then contrasted on the same models. Shifting a twin's read-out toward a control template corrects the disease signature, but the required correction is intrinsically distributed and no single-node edit reproduces it. A focal oscillatory drive at the most-deviant region fails at any amplitude, whereas a drive at the region selected by its effect on the disease discriminant succeeds from a single site. The two site sets are almost disjoint: the most-deviant regions are the most weakly connected, and therefore poor actuators, while effective sites are well-connected cortical nodes. Optimal targets are individual, and a closed-loop controller that titrates amplitude online from the model's own output reaches comparable efficacy at substantially lower dose. Demonstrated here on Alzheimer's disease, the framework is a general, model-based route to individualised target selection and dose minimisation.
